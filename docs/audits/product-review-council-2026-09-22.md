## 1. Executive launch verdict

CulinaryOS has implemented surfaces for restaurant sales, kitchen execution, management, recipes, inventory, purchasing, and public commerce, while JEV AI is described in the new brief but was not found by name in the repository search. Its strongest opportunity is a dependable daily loop connecting approved recipes and stock to prep, service, receiving, and end-of-day review. The largest workflow risk is that some interfaces and server paths represent success without sufficient authoritative evidence. Mobile risk comes from inconsistent shells and controls, including a fixed recipe sidebar and shared button defaults below the project's 48px target. AI trust risk comes from inconsistent flag enforcement, sample forecasting data, and usage reporting that does not establish actual provider consumption. Source-confirmed authorization and payment-replay defects block my recommendation for live multi-tenant, money-handling operation; deployed exposure remains unverified. The evidence supports internal testing and prototype review, with any real-customer pilot restricted to separately verified workflows after the relevant blockers close. Next, resolve the isolation, settlement, and dietary-certainty issues, then collect executable evidence for a deliberately narrow pilot rather than claiming ecosystem-wide production readiness.

## 5. UI/UX, workflow, and interaction audit

The archived images establish visible composition only. They cannot prove button behavior, current computed target size, successful integration or accessibility. “What works” below identifies a useful visible or specified foundation, not a passed usability test. Owners and confidence inherit the cited finding IDs.

**Onboarding and navigation — F14/F15/F17/F18.** Owner/operator goal: reach the first safe service with the correct location, menu and staff. A guided wizard and device-specific admin shells exist, which is a useful foundation. Hardcoded identity/counts undermine trust; the old screenshot's app names and ports expose architecture rather than jobs. Require Today, Menu & Recipes, Inventory & Purchasing, People, Reports, Settings in the management hub, with dedicated POS/KDS boundaries retained. Mobile uses a short bottom destination bar and resumable single-step forms; desktop uses grouped navigation and a setup checklist. Copy: “Next: verify your menu” with a specific incomplete prerequisite. Acceptance: fresh and returning users resume the same saved step after expiry; empty/partial setups show actionable guidance; restricted staff cannot open elevated APIs through direct links; labels/focus remain usable with keyboard and screen reader.

**POS/table/check/checkout — F03/F04/F07/F10.** Server/cashier goal: send exactly the intended order and settle supported tenders. The archived ticket keeps totals visible and Send to Kitchen at the bottom. Current code distinguishes an offline-queued message from approval, but server replay and acknowledgement defects defeat that distinction. Prioritize table/check identity, unsent changes, modifiers, totals and confirmed send/payment status; place Pay/Send in an unobscured bottom area on handhelds and ticket pane on tablets/desktops. Use “Saved on this device — awaiting sync” only after durable storage succeeds. Acceptance: retry/double-tap/refresh produces one intended operation; unsupported tenders stay unavailable; 200 responses containing operation failures cannot show paid; cancel restores the same ticket and focus; partial payment and reconnection retain an accurate remaining balance.

**KDS/service — F17/F19/F21.** Cook/expo goal: cook the right version, at the right station and course. The archived board visibly distinguishes fired/held work, elapsed timers and station filters. It does not prove duplicate prevention, accessible alerts or visibility at working distance. Make ticket age, table/order, station, course and critical instructions the hierarchy; group routine modifiers separately from allergy instructions and provide a non-color signal. Mobile opens one ticket detail; tablet/wall screens retain station overview with scalable density. Controls need Bump, recall/correction and explicit Fire Course semantics. Acceptance: replay does not duplicate a ticket; overdue and food-critical alerts remain visible until appropriately acknowledged; disconnect shows last update; keyboard/bump-bar and touch work; incoming work never steals input focus.

**Recipe/ingredient/scaling — F05/F13/F17.** Chef/prep goal: use an approved recipe with an explicit yield. Dedicated recipe navigation and a deterministic scaling engine are useful foundations. Numeric-prefix parsing, missing provenance and competing recipe surfaces threaten consistency. Show recipe version/status, yield/portion unit, ingredients and safety notes before photos; use Recipes rather than unexplained Vault terminology. Desktop supports detailed editing and revision comparison; tablet supports station use; phone offers readable steps, checked ingredients and a focused scaling sheet. Acceptance: draft/approved/active/archived states are distinct; fractions and very small quantities are calculated correctly; invalid values are rejected; rounding is explicit; edits cannot silently replace the version already assigned to a service; missing data is announced rather than interpreted as zero or safe.

**Menu/availability/publishing — F05/F10/F17.** Chef/manager goal: publish accurate items, prices and availability. Recipe/menu surfaces and 86 controls exist; current end-to-end publishing was not observed. Separate internal costing notes from guest copy and show the currently published revision next to draft changes. On mobile preserve item, price, location, channel, dietary status and change preview; defer only bulk layout work. Desktop provides side-by-side release comparison; tablet supports approval. Copy distinguishes “Save draft,” “Publish menu,” and “Mark unavailable.” Acceptance: price/allergen edits identify every affected public channel, require the authorized review and show propagation failure; queued availability is not presented as globally effective; offline conflicts retain both versions; focus returns to the changed item.

**Inventory/count/waste — F07/F11/F17.** Stockkeeper/prep goal: record a real quantity at a location in the correct unit. The pantry view includes quantities, units and replenishment thresholds. A long table alone does not support an interrupted store-room count. Lead with location/bin, item/pack, last counted time, current entry and unit; show par as context. Mobile uses a count route with next-item action, optional scan/photo and manual fallback; tablet supports count plus discrepancy; desktop reconciles variances. Acceptance: zero differs from not counted; damaged/rejected stock is separate; wrong barcode and duplicate scan are recoverable; a pending durable count survives refresh; storage failure is explicit; each adjustment preserves actor, reason, previous value and reversal history; errors are inline and announced.

**Purchasing/receiving — F01/F11/F17.** Buyer/receiver goal: obtain the right products and account for what arrived. The archived PO view exposes draft, line quantities, unit costs and totals. Current pantry success messages ignore rejection; the separate purchasing route has a different store and state vocabulary. Require one state contract: Suggested → Draft → Approved → Sending → Sent/Failed → Partially received/Received → Closed, with cancellation rules. Mobile review keeps supplier, total, pack, delivery date and changed prices visible; desktop compares catalogs; tablet/phone receiving records accepted/rejected/substituted quantities. Acceptance: approval never means transmission; retries send once; provider acknowledgement is recorded when applicable; 403/409/500 do not show success; partial receiving updates stock exactly once; confirmation focus and error summaries are accessible.

**Prep/tasks/checklists — F16/F17.** Prep lead/cook goal: execute assigned work and hand it over accurately. Prep pages and prompt templates establish the intended job; completion behavior is unverified. Hierarchy: task, station, quantity/unit, due time, critical instructions, assignee and status. Phone keeps Complete/Pause/Report issue at the bottom; tablet groups station work; desktop builds the production plan. Require completed, blocked, skipped-with-reason and reopened states rather than a single unchecked/checked flag. Acceptance: proof requirements are visible before completion; interrupted timers restore correctly; changed recipe versions trigger review; a reassigned task has one accountable owner; every critical instruction is text-accessible and not hidden behind hover.

**Catering/event/food-truck execution — F17/F18.** Operator/event lead goal: bring the correct food, equipment and people to the correct place. The brief specifies these needs; a complete current workflow was not established. Define an approved event revision with date/time zone, guest count, dietary requests, equipment, packing and owner. Desktop builds budgets/timeline; tablet runs the event; phone prioritizes load-out, contact, location, offline run sheet and change acknowledgement.
